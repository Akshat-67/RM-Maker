# ADR-0004: AI Extracts Facts, Templates Own Legal Language

## Status
Accepted

## Date
19-07-2026

## Context
Legal documents must contain precise legal phrasing approved by banking partners and government registrars. Using AI to generate the entire text of a deed introduces the risk of "hallucinations"—altering legal terms, omitting critical clauses, or introducing formatting errors.
However, manually typing the entire document is slow and error-prone.

## Decision
The document generation workflow is divided into two distinct responsibilities:
1. **AI Extracts Facts**: AI models (like Gemini) are responsible **only** for extracting structured factual parameters (e.g. names, dates, amounts, plot numbers) from source documents.
2. **Templates Own Legal Language**: The Master Word templates (`.docx`) contain the static legal phrasing, formatting styles, and Jinja placeholders. The template defines the exact phrasing of the document.
- The AI extractor must **never** output raw legal language or attempt to draft clauses.
- The compiler acts as a controller, mapping extracted facts to the template placeholders.

## Alternatives Considered
- **Direct AI Drafting**: Rejected. Generating the full deed using LLMs would fail quality checks, violate compliance policies, and introduce parsing errors.
- **Rules-Based Text Generation**: Rejected. Managing thousands of conditional clauses in Python code is highly complex and unmaintainable compared to using native Word templates with Jinja tags.

## Consequences
- **Positive**: Consistent, compliant document output. Legal formatting remains intact, and changing a clause only requires editing a Word file instead of modifying Python code.
- **Negative/Neutral**: Template placeholders must strictly match the schema contract, requiring disciplined naming conventions.

## Future Considerations
If banking requirements change, developers can swap out template files without altering the AI extraction schemas or the application's core code.
